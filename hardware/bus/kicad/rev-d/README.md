## The slot

Pins are numbered by position along the 36-pin socket, **counted from the GND end** (the socket itself is an unnumbered
strip: the numbers are the drawings', and each socket's footprint is turned so that its pin 1 is that end). **The order
since 2026-11-18** (Ken: "looking at the bus from the power supply end I want the right most pin to be gnd followed by
the next original pin order all the way to +5v/vcc, then EF1, EF2, EF3, -DMAin, SC0", -PAUSE kept, at the far end; and
"start counting at gnd as pin 1, there +5v is 30 and then the pins beyond 5v"):

| Positions | Lines |
|---|---|
| 1–30 | the 30-pin System-5 bus from its GND end: GND at 1, the end pin (the rightmost, seen from the power connector), N0 2, N1 3, -RESET 4, -VMA 5, -EF 6, N2 7, -INT 8, A7–A0 at 9–16, -MRW 17, -MRD 18, TPB 19, TPA 20, Q 21, D7–D0 at 22–29, +5V at 30. A 30-pin card's own pin *n* (+5V 1 … GND 30) is position 31 − *n* |
| 31–33 | -EF1, -EF2, -EF3 (-EF1 next to +5V) |
| 34–36 | -DMAIN, SC0, -PAUSE (-PAUSE at the far end: the leftmost, seen from the power connector) |

A 30-pin card plugs into positions 1–30, flush with the GND end. Until 2026-11-18 the slot was counted from the other
end, in the 2009 board's order: 1–3 -EF1, -EF2, -EF3, 4–33 the 30-pin bus (+5V at 4, GND at 33), 34–36 -PAUSE, -DMAIN,
SC0 (the 2009 drawing's -INP, -OUT, EXP), the 30-pin bus in the middle. So a 36-pin card for this order (CPU Rev D, the
IO panel, BUSDRV-C) **does not fit the 2009 backplane**: there its +5V pin would meet D2. A 30-pin card fits both. The
2009 board's Eagle pin numbers differ too (1–30 the bus, 31–33 -EF1–3, 34–36 the rest), and its copper wired -EF1 next
to +5V, as here now, although its silkscreen says -EF3. The bus lines themselves, the pull-ups and the slots' places
are as before: only which line runs along which row changed, with RN1 (in line with the +5V and D0–D7 rows) and the
card-window brackets, and RN3 and RN4 moved to make room for RN1.

## Circuit

| Part | What |
|---|---|
| J1–J8 | the slots: 36-pin single-row 0.1" sockets on a 1.0" (25.4 mm) pitch, as 2009, but slots 7 and 8 1.25" (31.75 mm) apart (Ken, 2026-11-18) |
| J9 | power in: a DC barrel jack, Same Sky (formerly CUI) **PJ-063AH** (right angle, 2.0 mm centre pin for the common 5.5 × 2.1 mm plug, 8 A, 24 V; Digi-Key and Mouser stock it), at the left edge with its opening flush with the edge. **Centre positive**, 7–15 V: a 9 V or 12 V adapter of 2 A or more is the usual choice (a 7 V one at most about 2 A of load: below). Pin 1 the centre pin, pin 2 the sleeve (GND); its two kinked support legs (MP) are soldered to GND. Labelled J9 POWER IN, 7-15V DC, CENTER + |
| D2 | reverse-polarity protection: a Schottky rectifier, Vishay **SB540-E3/54** (40 V, 5 A, DO-201AD; the onsemi SB540 is obsolete), in series from J9's centre pin, lying flat (5.3 mm body). A centre-negative plug leaves it reverse-biased, so nothing reaches C10, the regulator or the cards. It drops about 0.4 V at 2 A (about 0.8 W), which the regulator absorbs |
| C10 | 47 µF 35 V, 6.3 mm can, on the regulator's input side (after D2, so a reversed plug never reaches it): it damps the ring when an adapter is plugged in live, through its cable's inductance |
| SW1 | power switch: an **M9040P**, the upright Marquardt SPDT toggle of the IO panel's data switches and of the YACC1 IO card (Ken, 2026-11-18: "bus change on off switch to same toggle used on IO card data 1-8"; earlier that day a right-angle Marquardt 9070-1W, YACC1's RESET/RUN model, which a real switch fitted on the full-size print, and from 2026-10-27 an E-Switch 100 series). Footprint `s5bus.pretty/M9040P`, the IO panel's (the YACC1 IO card's, pad for pad): three oval pads 5.41 × 2.71 mm, 4.699 mm apart, 1.8 mm holes (its body's outline on the fab layer at full size since 2026-11-19, Ken: "yes draw the toggle outline full size"; until then that layer had only the sketch of the lever, 8.2 × 10.3 mm); a body of 8.6 × 13.4 mm standing on the board below J10, 10.9 mm in from the left edge, its lever moving along that edge. It follows the source jumper and switches whichever source JP1 picks: common (pin 2) from JP1's middle pin; pin 3 to +5V = ON with the lever **up**, towards J9 (a toggle closes the contact on the side away from its lever: check against a real switch); pin 1 open = OFF. U1 runs whenever an adapter is in J9, the switch only cutting its output from the bus. Labelled ON, SW1 POWER and OFF beside it, each position level with the end its lever is at. **Its contact rating:** the whole bus current passes through it (up to 3 A from U1, or what J10's supply gives). Distributors list Marquardt's standard 9040-series toggles at 5 A 48 V AC and about 4 A 30 V DC, which covers it; the series' low-level (gold contact) versions are for 0.4 VA only and would not do. The data sheets themselves could not be read here on 2026-11-18: check the part number on the switch that is fitted |
| U1 | the 5 V regulator: TRACO **TSR 3-2450N**, a switching (buck) regulator in a SIP-3 with a 7805's pinout (1 in, 2 GND, 3 out): 5 V ± 2 % at up to 3 A from 6.5–36 V, 92 % efficient, no heat sink, short-circuit protected (it limits at about 2.4 times its rating and recovers by itself: Ken chose no fuse), at most 820 µF of capacitance on its output (C1 and the cards' own bulk capacitors, about 10 µF each, stay well below it; no card should add a large one). Its data sheet: `../../../../../private-resources/elf/reference/datasheets/tracopower-tsr3n-datasheet.pdf` (TRACO, Rev. 2026-10-08). KiCad's own symbol and footprint (`Converter_DCDC:TSR3-2450N`, `Converter_DCDC_TRACO_TSR2-24xxN_TSR2-24xxxN_THT`, whose outline matches the TSR 3N's: 14 × 7.6 mm, 10.2 mm tall, the pins 6.16 mm from its back; KiCad 10 has no 3D model for it, so the render shows its outline). It stands upright left of x = 23.7 mm, below C10, clear of the card in slot 1, with no track or via under it (TRACO: avoid routing under the converter); the GND pour fills under it. Its output goes to JP1's REG pin, round the right of its body. At 12 V in and a 2.5 A load it takes about 1.1 A |
| J10 | an external regulated 5 V (Ken, 2026-11-18): two screw terminals, Phoenix Contact **MKDS 1,5/2-5,08** (5.08 mm pitch, wire entry parallel to the board; KiCad's `TerminalBlock_Phoenix_MKDS-1,5-2-5.08_1x02_P5.08mm_Horizontal`), at the left edge below U1 with its wire entry facing out (seen in the render from that edge). **Pin 1 +5V, pin 2 GND**, labelled +5V and GND beside them. **Nothing protects this input**: no diode (it would take 0.3 V or more off the 5 V), no fuse; a supply connected the wrong way round or set above 5.25 V reaches every card. A regulated 5 V supply of 3 A or more, current-limited if it can be. Its GND runs on the top side, under SW1, to C1 and the slots' GND row |
| JP1 | the 5 V source (Ken, 2026-11-18): a 1 × 3 pin header (0.1", 8.5 mm tall with its shunt) under U1 with **one shunt**: pins 1-2 **REG** (U1), pins 2-3 **EXT** (J10); no shunt, no power to the bus. Pin 2 goes to SW1's common. One shunt cannot join U1's output to an external supply. Labelled REG and EXT at its ends and JP1 5V SOURCE. The whole bus current passes through the shunt: a common one is rated about 2–3 A, so for a heavy load use a good one, or a wire link soldered across the two pins in place of the header |
| C1 | 220 µF 16 V bulk on +5V (after the switch, so on either source), 6.3 mm can, 2.5 mm leads, at the foot of the +5V riser below SW1 |
| C2–C9 | 0.1 µF, one above each slot, 0.1" leads |
| D1, R1 | power LED (left edge) and its 470 Ω |
| RN1 | 10k × 8 SIP-9 on D0–D7, common to +5V: stands in line with the +5V and D0–D7 rows (positions 30–22), its pins on those bus tracks |
| RN2 | 10k × 8 SIP-9 (bussed, as RN1) on -EF, -EF1, -EF2, -EF3, -PAUSE, -DMAIN, -INT, -RESET |
| RN3 | 10k × 5 SIP-6 (bussed, common to GND) on N0, N1, N2, TPA, TPB |
| RN4 | 10k × 3 SIP-4 (bussed, common to +5V) on -VMA, -MRD, -MRW |
| H1–H8 | M3 mounting holes, each with no copper within 4 mm of its centre on either side |

-INT and -RESET are pulled up here too (Ken, 2026-10-31). The CPU card has its own (R7, and the CM1232's reset output with R3), but with the CPU card out (bench tests from the bus driver, whose -RESET is open-collector) a floating -RESET held the 6845 in reset and blanked its screen. The two pull-ups in parallel are harmless.

## Layout

- 2 layers, 244.35 × 116 mm (238 × 114 until 2026-11-18: slot 8 moved out 6.35 mm, and 2 mm more at the GND end, below). Each bus line is one straight track on the bottom, from the leftmost part on it across all eight slots; +5V and GND 1.5 mm, the rest 0.8 mm. The top is a GND pour (one piece; every GND pin reaches it).
- **Supply feeds:** all the cards' current comes in through J9 or J10, on locked tracks, down the left end in the order J9, D2 and C10, U1, J10 and JP1, SW1, C1, so that no supply track crosses another (2026-11-18). Bottom side, **2.0 mm**: J9's centre pin to D2's anode; D2's cathode across to C10's + and down into U1's Vin; U1's Vout out above its body, round its right side and down to JP1 pin 1 (REG); J10 pin 1 straight across to JP1 pin 3 (EXT); JP1 pin 2 down to SW1's common; SW1 pin 3 out to the riser at x 25, up into the +5V row, with C1's + at its foot. Top side, inside the GND pour, **2.5 mm**: J9's sleeve past C10's − into U1's GND, from above; J10's GND past SW1's left side to C1's −, and on down into the slots' GND row. The GND pins of J9, C10, U1, J10 and C1 join the pour solidly (no thermal spokes). No track or via under U1's body. The tall parts (J9, C10, U1, J10, SW1 and C1, about 11 mm) stand left of x = 23.7 mm, clear of a card in slot 1, whose components face them (up to 21.3 mm from it); JP1 (8.5 mm with its shunt) to x 24.2; D2 lies flat. RN2 stands left of slot 1, about 12 mm clear of its socket (Ken, 2026-11-18: at x 40 it stood 2.6 mm from it), its legend on its right; RN3 and RN4 in the corner above RN1, right of D2 (since 2026-11-18: RN1 moved down with the +5V row onto their old place). **Voltages:** U1 holds +5V at 5.0 V ± 2 %; the riser and the +5V row drop about 0.1 V to slot 8 at 2.5 A (1 oz copper), so slot 8 sees about 4.9 V: measure it there under load. On the input side D2 drops about 0.4 V and U1 needs 6.5 V, so J9 wants 7 V or more: a 7 V adapter suits a light load (to about 2 A), a 9 V or 12 V one any load
- **Mounting holes:** a 4 mm-radius keep-out (no track, no pour, both sides) around each hole, so a metal screw head, washer or standoff touches only bare board; the nearest track's copper edge is 4.26 mm from a hole's centre (its centreline 4.76 mm; measured from the board file 2026-11-27). The board is 116 mm tall. It grew from 112 to 114 so the bottom holes' keep-outs clear the bottom row, and to 116 on 2026-11-18 (Ken) for the 30-pin cards: plugged in flush with the GND end, such a card (its connector 11.3 mm from its edge: memory Rev E) reaches 11.3 mm past the GND row, and the board now 12.1 mm, where 114 left the card 1.2 mm over the backplane's edge.
- **Card window:** on each slot a silkscreen bracket marks positions 1–30, where a 30-pin card goes (flush with the slot's GND end), with a filled triangle at the +5V end. A single-row socket would take a card shifted along it, or turned round (its +5V pin onto the backplane's GND).
- **Component side:** the cards' components face the power connector (left, towards J9; Ken, 2026-10-27). An arrow marked COMPONENT SIDE under each slot shows it.
- The power area is at the left end (Ken's board-layout rules for new boards: the power connector and LED at an edge, connectors facing out, every part labelled outside its body).
- Every slot pin is labelled on the silkscreen with its signal; each slot is labelled `Jn SLOT n`. All silkscreen text is at least 1.0 mm tall with a 0.18 mm stroke (JLCPCB's minimum: 1.0 mm / 0.153 mm).
- Fab files: plated and non-plated holes in separate drill files (`s5bus-d-PTH.drl`, `s5bus-d-NPTH.drl`: the eight 3.2 mm mounting holes and J9's 1.6 mm locating peg, 9 of the 368 holes; J9's pins and legs are plated slots); the drill maps stay in `gerbers/` and are left out of the zip.
- **A full-size print** (Ken, 2026-11-18: "how can i print bus board 1:1 so i can measure things like switch"): `s5bus-d-1to1.pdf`, the board at 1:1 on one US Letter sheet, lying: the outline, every pad with its hole at its real size, the parts' outlines and labels, no tracks. Print it at 100 % (Preview: Scale 100 %, not "Scale to Fit") and measure the 100 mm line under the board; then lay the real parts on their pads (SW1, J9, J10, U1). `gen/build.sh` remakes it with the board (`tools/kicad/print_1to1.py`, for any board).
- **To check when building:** with a meter, unpowered: about 0 Ω from J9's centre pin to D2's anode, from D2's cathode to U1's Vin (pin 1), from U1's Vout (pin 3) to JP1 pin 1 and from J10's +5V terminal to JP1 pin 3; with the shunt on JP1 1-2, from U1's Vout to SW1's common, and with the lever up (towards J9) from there to slot 1's position 30 (+5V); lever down, open. Then with no cards, a 9 V or 12 V adapter (centre positive), the shunt on REG and the lever up: D1 lights and slot 1's positions 30 (+5V) and 1 (GND) read 4.9-5.1 V. Then the adapter out, the shunt on EXT and a bench supply at 5.0 V, current-limited, on J10 (+5V and GND as labelled): D1 lights, the same reading. Measure the cards' total draw once: U1 gives 3 A, and above that it limits and recovers by itself.
