# Bus board Rev D: theory of operation

The bus Rev D (S5BUS-D) is the 2026 backplane: eight 36-pin slots, its own 5 V regulator, and resistors that hold every bus line at a safe level when nothing drives it. [bus.md](bus.md) defines the signals, the slot order and the 36-pin extension, and covers the Rev D board in outline. This chapter is about the physical board only.

- Board status: **drawn and routed, not ordered, not built.** Two layers, 244.35 × 116 mm. `pcb-check --tests` passed at elf `e5322e4` (2026-11-19: no failures, 0 warnings), before `test_busboard.py` (below) joined the board's tests line on 2026-11-25.
- KiCad: `hardware/bus/kicad/rev-d/`, generated by `gen/build.sh` from `gen/netlist.py` (the circuit), `gen/make_board.py` and `gen/route.py`. The redrawn schematic is `schematic/s5bus-d-schematic.pdf`. `s5bus-d-1to1.pdf` is the board at full size, for laying the real parts on.
- The full record: `hardware/bus/kicad/rev-d/README.md`.

## What changed from the 2009 board, and why

The machine runs today on Ken's 2009 backplane (`hardware/bus/kicad/rev-2009/`): six 36-pin slots, every pin bussed, nothing else. Its power connector reaches GND only, so +5 V comes in by a wire, and it has no pull-ups. Rev D changes these things:

- **Eight slots, not six**, enough for CPU, memory, video, IO, IDE, the bus driver and two spares.
- **A new slot order** (2026-11-18). Positions are counted from the GND end. The 30-pin bus is at 1-30 and the six extra lines are beyond +5V. A 30-pin card plugs in flush with the GND end. **The 36-pin cards drawn for this order do not fit the 2009 board**, where their +5V pin would meet D2.
- **Power on the board:** a barrel jack and a regulator, or an external 5 V, a power switch and a power LED.
- **Pull-ups and pull-downs** on every line that must idle in a known state. With the CPU card out, a floating -RESET held the 6845 in reset (2026-10-31), and the 2009 board's data lines float.

## The slots

The slots are 1.0 in (25.4 mm) apart, as on the 2009 board, which leaves 21.3 mm of component height for each card. **Slots 7 and 8 are 1.25 in (31.75 mm) apart**, so the card in slot 8 has 27.6 mm. Each slot's pins are named on the silkscreen, and each slot is labelled `Jn SLOT n`.

A single-row socket would take a card shifted along it or turned round. Two marks guard against this. A silkscreen bracket marks positions 1-30 on each slot, with a filled triangle at the +5V end, for a 30-pin card. An arrow, COMPONENT SIDE, shows that the cards' components face the power end (J9). The pinout itself is in [bus.md](bus.md), "The 36-pin extension".

## Power

The supply runs down the left end in order, so no feed crosses another: J9 → D2 → C10 → U1 → JP1 (REG), or J10 → JP1 (EXT); then JP1's middle pin → SW1 → +5V, with C1 and the PWR LED after the switch.

- **From an adapter:** 7-15 V, centre positive; a 9 V or 12 V adapter of 2 A or more is the usual choice (D2 drops about 0.4 V and U1 needs 6.5 V). U1 gives 3 A, limits a short and recovers by itself, so there is no fuse. It allows at most 820 µF on its output: no card should add a large capacitor.
- **From an external 5 V on J10:** nothing protects this input. A supply connected the wrong way round, or set above 5.25 V, reaches every card. Use a regulated, current-limited supply.
- **JP1** takes one shunt, so the two sources can never be joined. With no shunt the bus has no power. The whole bus current passes through the shunt; for a heavy load, solder a wire link across the two pins instead.
- **SW1** carries the whole bus current. Its rating is not yet confirmed (below).
- **Voltage drop:** the +5V row is 1.5 mm wide, about 0.33 Ω a metre in 1 oz (35 µm) copper, and slot 8 is 184 mm from slot 1: 2.5 A drawn at slot 8 would drop about 0.15 V along it, less when the load is spread over the slots. Measure slot 8 under load.

## Termination and idle states

The board has no AC termination (no series or RC parts on any line). Its resistors only set what each line reads when nothing drives it:

- **Pull-ups on D0-D7** (RN1): a read with no card answering gives FF.
- **Pull-ups on the open-collector lines** (RN2). Several cards may pull these. The CPU card adds its own 10k on the same lines, which is harmless in parallel.
- **Idle with no master** (RN3, RN4): N0-N2, TPA and TPB low; -VMA, -MRD and -MRW high. With the CPU card out, no I/O port is selected, no strobe is active, and no card drives the data bus or writes memory. The bus driver relies on this when it is the master.

## Layout

Each bus line is one straight track on the bottom layer across all eight slots (+5V and GND 1.5 mm, the rest 0.8 mm); the top layer is one GND pour. The tall power parts stand left of slot 1's card, with no track under U1. No copper lies within 4 mm of a mounting hole's centre (about 2.4 mm from its edge), so a metal screw touches only bare board. The board is 116 mm tall so that a 30-pin card flush with the GND end stays over it.

## Testing

- `software/busdrv/busboard.py` and `test_busboard.py` (2026-11-25) read the board from its KiCad netlist (`reports/netlist.net`) and plug five cards into its sockets by pin, each from its own netlist: CPU Rev D, memory Rev E, the 6845 card Rev B-Clean-Keyboard, the IO panel and IDE/CF Rev A. A program that uses every bus line runs and is compared cycle by cycle with the instruction-level emulator. The tests plant faults in the board and check that each one is noticed: every slot position open (288), neighbours swapped (280), a slot turned or moved one along (16), neighbouring lines shorted (35), the pull resistors, and the supply path. It found one power-up fault (2026-11-25, `ffe6409`): the IO panel's REQ flip-flop had no state at power-up, since put right ([io-panel.md](io-panel.md)).
- `software/busdrv/bringup.py bus36`: on the built board, the bus driver BUSDRV-C alone checks the pull-ups, the pull-downs and the six extension lines. `bringup.py shorts`, the short finder, is meant for a bare backplane after its 288 socket joints are soldered (`software/busdrv/BRINGUP.md`). Both run only in the simulator so far.
- The `tests =` line of `pcb-check.ini` runs `test_bringup` and `test_busboard`. Its `bus_pins` line checks all eight slots, position by position, against `tools/s5bus.py`.

## Not yet proven on hardware

- **SW1's contact rating** against up to 3 A. The standard 9040-series toggles are listed at about 4 A DC, but the gold low-level versions are rated 0.4 VA only. Check the part number on the switch that is fitted. Also check its ON direction (drawn as lever up, towards J9).
- The card supports: where they go, now that a 30-pin card sits 7.6 mm towards the GND end of a 36-pin card's centre line.
- The cards' total current against U1's 3 A.
- The socket height against the card guides.
